New here? Start here.

This tool reads your bank statement and turns it into organised business records. You do not need to know any accounting. There are five short steps:

  1. Upload your bank statement.
  2. Organise: every payment is sorted into a category, like fuel or advertising.
  3. Review: check the ones we were unsure about. Fix any that are wrong and the tool remembers.
  4. Your finances: see your profit, cash flow and what to look into, explained in plain English.
  5. Download reports you can give your accountant.

How do I get my statement?

In your bank's app or internet banking, open the account, choose statements or transaction history, pick the dates you want, and download the file. CSV or Excel is the most accurate. PDF also works if it is not a scan or a photo.

Profit is sales and customer payments minus business expenses. Tax payments, owner withdrawals and uncategorised items are left out until you review them.

What do these terms mean?
  • Money received and spent: everything that entered or left your bank account, including things that are not business income or costs.
  • Business expenses: money spent running the business, such as stock, ads, software and fuel. It leaves out tax payments and money you moved to yourself.
  • Operating profit: sales minus business expenses. It is what the business earned from running day to day, before income tax.
  • Cash flow: the difference between money coming in and going out over a period. A business can be profitable and still run short of cash.
  • Uncategorised: payments the tool could not sort yet. Until you sort them, your profit figure may be wrong.
  • VAT: a 15% tax added to most sales. If your business is VAT-registered, you collect it for SARS and can claim back VAT you paid on business costs, but only with valid tax invoices.
  • Provisional tax: advance payments of income tax during the year, based on an estimate of your profit.
  • Owner withdrawals: money you take out for yourself. It is not a business expense.
